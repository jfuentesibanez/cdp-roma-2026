# Illustration prompts

Four illustrations for the Rome deck. Generate them with GPT Images (or similar), square (1:1),
and save each one in `img/` under the file name in its heading. The deck picks them up on its
own: the image appears on the right of the slide and the text narrows to make room. While a
file is missing, the slide looks exactly as it does now.

1. Generate `art-ladder` first. It sets the look.
2. Keep it in the conversation and attach it as style reference for the rest: *"same
   illustrator, same palette, same line weight as the attached image"*.
3. The background must be the flat light grey given below, edge to edge. The deck blends it
   into the slide; a white or tinted background shows as a visible square.

## STYLE BLOCK: paste this ahead of every subject

> Editorial illustration. Hand-drawn ink line work with loose watercolour washes, in the
> tradition of classic European press illustration: visible pencil under the ink, slightly
> uneven line weight. The washes sit a little off the ink lines instead of filling them
> exactly, the way real watercolour does. Restrained cool palette: slate blue-grey #2E3B48 for
> the ink and the darkest washes, soft warm greys and pale sand for the rest, and one single
> accent colour per image, named in the subject. Quiet, elegant, a lot of empty space. The
> objects belong to an Italian office of the 1960s: wood, enamelled steel, paper, cloth.
> Flat plain background in light grey #F6F7F8, edge to edge, no gradient, no vignette, no cast
> shadow on the background, no ground plane, no frame or border. One subject, at most one
> prop, nothing else in the scene. Absolutely no text, letters, numerals, logos or watermarks.
> No real person's face. Square format, print quality.

## img/art-ladder.png
Slide "The broken ladder"

A tall wooden ladder leaning against nothing, seen slightly from the side. The upper rungs are
intact and well worn. The three lowest rungs are snapped: splintered stubs still fixed to the
rails, one broken rung lying on the floor beside it. Nobody on the ladder. Accent: the intact
upper rungs carry a thin wash of green #00924A; the broken ones are bare grey wood. Mood: calm,
a little unsettling. You can stand at the top, but nobody can start the climb.

## img/art-chair.png
Slide "Your AI project will change someone's job. Let's find out whose, and how."

A 1960s swivel office chair seen three-quarters from behind, a jacket hung over its back as if
its owner has just stepped away. On the seat, a small neat stack of paper folders. No person,
no desk. Accent: the jacket in green #00924A; everything else in slate and grey. Mood: someone
real sits here, and they will be back.

## img/art-stamp.png
Slide "Every change meets resistance. Who will resist your project, and why?"

A close view of one hand and forearm in a shirt cuff, gripping a wooden-handled rubber stamp
firmly, held just above a sheet of blank paper. The knuckles are tight: the hand is not
stamping, it is holding on. No face, no body beyond the forearm. The paper and the stamp face
are blank. Accent: the stamp's ink pad edge and a smudge on the fingertips in red #D0202E.
Mood: this signature is mine.

## img/art-crate.png
Slide "Your MVP works. Today you plan how it gets used."

A compact 1960s desktop calculating machine, an invented design with rounded enamelled casing
and a row of blank keys, sitting half unpacked inside an open wooden shipping crate, wood wool
packing spilling over the edge, the lid leaning against the side. No cables connected, nobody
around. The machine is not a copy of any real product. Accent: the machine's casing in green
#00924A; crate and packing in sand and grey. Mood: finished, delivered, and still in the box.
